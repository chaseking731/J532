Meet Corey Kispert

27 Year Old Atlanta Hawks Forward

Gonzaga Bulldog Legend

Before Corey Kispert was a four-year starter at Gonzaga and Naismith Men's College Player of the Year finalist, he was just a three-star forward prospect from Seattle, Washington. He went to nearby Gonzaga University, where he played behind legendary college basketball head coach Mark Few. Few saw something in the young Kispert that was pretty obvious: He was a good basketball player. He played in 35 games in his freshman year and started seven of them. That isn't very typical for a three-star prospect on a great team, but this is Corey Kispert we are talking about. Kispert averaged 6.7 points per game (PPG) and finished with a 35.1% three-point percentage (3P%). He would only miss one start in the next three years. In his sophomore year, his role was still small, but he averaged 8 PPG on 37.4 3P% anyway. His junior year, he was unleashed, and he averaged 13.9 PPG on a ridiculous 43.8 3P%. His senior year, however, was when he became a different beast. He finished with 18.6 PPG, while maintaining a 44.0 3P%, 52.9 field goal percentage (FG%), and a 67.4 true-shooting percentage (TS%). A 67.4 TS% with a 52.4 three-point rate is unbelievable. He also finished 10th in the nation in boxscore plus-minus (BMI), a very important value index.


Beginning of NBA career

Now, of course, because Kispert isn’t some sort of elite athlete–and maybe also because the GMs around the league aren’t sure what they are doing–he was doubted entering the draft. Seriously, they doubted a dude with 10.4 BMI and 44.0 3P% on 52.4 3PR. He fell all the way to the Washington Wizards at pick 15 and would instantly become a good role player. He had 8.2 PPG as a rookie with a 35.0 3P% and 45.5 FG%. But more impressively, he brought stability and spacing to the offense. He finished his rookie year with a +5.0 NET on-off ranking, meaning the team was 5.0 NET ratings better when he was on the court— as a rookie! He would continue to impact the team positively within his role throughout his time in Washington. He would rise to a 42.4 3P% in his second year in the league, which is absurd. The 42.4 3P% would not be consistently upheld throughout his career, but he has and will always be a good shooter. He would even become a plus-defender throughout his time in Washington, and the Wizards would see a better defensive NET rating when he was on the court through the end of his run there. ram, which would allow him to pursue his dream of sports writing.


New Beginnings

Kispert was used in a trade for Trae Young, a point guard once viewed as a superstar, and now viewed as a great offensive player but a defensive liability. The Wizards bought low and only sent over an old guard to go with Kispert. While most Wizards fans are ecstatic about the move, I think we should have given up anybody but Corey Kispert. Kispert has been good for the Hawks. He is averaging 10 points per game and scored 33 points against the Wizards. Which is good. He deserved to do that.

Future

The future is bright for the young superstar (in my heart). He is only 27 years old, and his play style would allow him to play into his mid-to-late 30s. Hopefully, at some point in time, he will finally get to play for a real basketball team with championship aspirations. He yearns for championship basketball.
